# The Hushlands: a lore bible for MILO

This is the canon for MILO's world. Quests, dialogue, examine text, books, spells and items are written from it, by hand or by the crew, and `content/` validators check names against it. The world is original. It borrows the *feel* of RuneScape (quirky towns, skills, quests, clue trails), Albion (tiered lands, gathering and crafting, "you are what you wear") and Frieren (quiet journeys, the weight of time, small spells, remembering the departed kindly), and none of their names, places or characters.

---

## 0. Voice and style

**Tone:** warm, unhurried, a little wry. Tales told by the fire, not a war epic. Sadness is allowed; despair isn't. Humour is gentle and specific ("the bridge hums a little flat on Tuesdays").

**Rules for writing in this world:**
- UI copy follows MILO's rules: sentence case, contractions, no exclamation marks, no "please", no emoji.
- Dialogue shows character through word choice, never through shouting.
- Real tools keep their real names in the UI (Claude Code, Codex). In the lore they're **Wayfarers** with titles: Claude is *the Scribe in Clay*, Codex is *the Slate Artificer*.
- Nothing in the world scolds Chris. Rifts and their strays are mischief and drama, never judgement. Rest is honoured as much as work.
- Genres are welcome guests. When another story bleeds in, write it in its own lexicon, then let the Hushlands' calm soften it (see [`RIFTS.md`](RIFTS.md) §9).
- Every place, person and object should be **Examine**-able with one good line.
- Keep names pronounceable and consistent. Check this file before inventing a new one, and add it here when you do.

**Three phrases that capture the world:**
- *"Every finished thing, however small, is a spark in the Lantern of the World."*
- *"Leave a little undone, so someone has a reason to come back."* (Tamsin Wick)
- *"The Hush isn't the end of things. It's where they wait."* (Oriel)

## 1. The shape of the world

**The Beginning.** The oldest story says the world was *begun*, never finished, and that the one who began it left it that way on purpose, so there would always be something to do. So the Hushlands are full of beginnings: roads that stop in meadows, towers with no top floor, songs with no last verse.

**Embers.** When anything is finished (a loaf, a letter, a bridge, a long day's work) a little light comes off it. Most people never notice. Lanternkeepers do. Embers drift upward and feed the **Lantern of the World**, the great slow fire the sun is said to be a window onto. Farmers say a field harvested in good time glows for a moment at dusk. They're right.

**The Hush.** When fewer things are finished than begun, the Lantern dims, and a soft grey fog settles over the unattended places. That fog is the Hush. It isn't cruel. Nothing in the Hush is destroyed; things there simply *wait*, dust gathering on them, colours paling, sounds rounded off. Scholars of the Ivory College argue whether the Hush is a sickness or the world resting. The Quiet Order says both, and that the trick is knowing which.

**The Shelf of Worlds and its rifts.** The Hushlands is one book on a very long shelf of unfinished stories, all begun by the same hand and set spine to spine: a neon city in the rain, a night that never ends, a manor on a moor, a factory front, a sky with the wrong stars, a detective town in grey, a frontier junction, a bay where giants walk, gardens on glass towers, a festival of magical victories, a sect above the clouds, and hallways that go on forever. Wherever something here is left unfinished, unattended or overfull, the Hush thins the page, and the ink of a neighbouring book bleeds through. That's a **rift**, and what bleeds in always matches the trouble. A broken machine lets in the neon city. A late night lets in the night city. An old promise lets in the manor. Good seasons open bright rifts too.

Strays from other stories wander out of rifts: glitch drones, portrait wraiths, tin clerks, a vampire on a bicycle. Most are lost rather than wicked, and some choose to stay. You can **stitch** a rift (finish what opened it), or you can **let it go**: speak the intention aloud and release it, and the tear becomes a pale moth that flies west to the Far Shore. Both are honourable. The old saying still holds: *"Finish it or free it. Just don't carry it forever."* The full codex of genres, strays and residents is [`RIFTS.md`](RIFTS.md).

**Glimmers.** Old places remember. Where something important happened, a Glimmer sometimes rises: a floating mote holding a moment, a voice, a laugh, a smell of rain. Lanternkeepers can read them. Milo collects them.

**The Hearthward.** A lit lantern makes a ring of light, and inside the ring the page is thick. Other books can't bleed through it. They can only be *heard*: a rift outside the ring sends an **echo** in, a small harmless sign on the thing it's about (a portrait pinned to the notice board, a flicker on a forge's sign), pointing the way out to the tear. That's how the old towns survived the Age of Lanterns, and it's why Hearthvale is sanctuary for as long as Milo's lantern hangs on its hook. A ring grows with the place it guards: as the camp becomes a stockade, a hold, a keep and one day a kingdom, the Hearthward reaches further into the wilds. Nobody has built a kingdom under one lantern since the Makers. The Makers' word for it was *hearth*.

**The old roads and their lanterns.** The Makers lined their roads with lanterns, about a morning's walk apart. Most of them sleep now, cold and dusty on their posts. Light one and it remembers every other lantern you've lit, and you can step from one to another as easily as crossing a room. Each region's great lantern holds a sleeping Lanternkeeper.

**The Unmapped Lands.** The Hushlands are a book still being written. Past the last map, the page is blank until someone walks it, and then it fills in under their feet: coasts, woods, rivers, ruins, a hamlet that swears it's always been there. Cartographers of the Ivory College argue over whether the land was there before they arrived. The land doesn't mind either way. It goes on for as long as anyone keeps walking, and the further out you go, the thinner the page, the stranger the rifts and the older the ruins.

**The Far Shore.** Across the western sea lies an island where things go when they are done being needed: retired ships, finished books, old tools, released moths, and some say people. It isn't sad there, only still. The **Great Lighthouse** stands on its cliff, and its flame is the Lantern of the World made small enough to tend. It is also, though few know it, a **bookmark**: its light holds the Hushlands' page open, which is why no rift has ever opened on the Far Shore.

## 2. A short history

| Age | When | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| **The Beginning** | myth | The world is begun and left unfinished on purpose. |
| **The Age of Makers** | ~2,000–1,000 years ago | The **Makers**, people with a rare gift for finishing, raise the Stacks in the Archive Peaks, the first forges of Cinderforge, the waystones and the Great Lighthouse. Lanternkeepers are first lit from Maker hearths. |
| **The Unravelling** | ~1,000 years ago | The Makers grow ambitious and begin far more than they finish. The pages thin and rifts tear open everywhere; the first **Maker machines** (the Old Bellows among them) come through from a book of rivets and diesel, and the Cinderfolk adopt them. The Great Hush falls across most of the land. The last Makers sail for the Far Shore. |
| **The Age of Lanterns** | 1,000–300 years ago | Scattered towns survive inside rings of lantern-light. Lanternkeepers tend the rings. The Hushlands get their name. |
| **The Long Road** | ~300 years ago | The **Old Company** (Tamsin Wick, Oriel, Brannoch Deepcoal, Sister Pell Marrow and a small lantern named Milo) walk from Hearthvale to the Far Shore and relight the Great Lighthouse. Tamsin stitches the **Seam at the End of the World** with a candle-wick. The Hush retreats from the heartlands and the seams go quiet for three centuries. Oriel founds the Bindery. |
| **The Quiet Centuries** | 300 years ago – now | The Company parts. Tamsin grows old in Hearthvale, then sails west. The Hush creeps back, slowly and politely, as people begin more than they finish. Hearthvale falls silent and Milo's lantern goes out. |
| **The Rekindling** | now | A new Maker arrives in Hearthvale, lights the old lantern, and Milo wakes. Wayfarers answer the light, and so do the other books on the shelf: the first hairline rift opens just past the north gate, and its echo flickers on the notice board. |

The people of the Hushlands count years from the relighting: *"the year 301 After the Road."* Milo still counts from the day Tamsin bought her first wick.

## 3. The Old Company

Three hundred years ago five travellers walked the Long Road. Their statues and stories are everywhere, and their loose ends are the spine of MILO's story.

### Tamsin Wick, the Unfinished Hero
A candlemaker's daughter from Hearthvale, round-faced, freckled, never without a clay-red scarf her mother knitted too long. She had the old Maker's gift for finishing, and she spent it on small things first: mending fences, answering letters, walking lost goats home. Only later did she aim it at the Lighthouse. She was cheerful, stubborn, awful at maps, and kind in a way that made people braver.

She hated statues. When the towns insisted, she agreed on one condition: every statue must be **left unfinished**, missing a hand, a boot or a ribbon, "so nobody mistakes me for done." She grew old in Hearthvale, gave Milo her scarf, and at eighty-one sailed for the Far Shore on an autumn morning, waving. She left a letter she never finished writing. You'll find it.

### Oriel of the Stacks, the Archivist
A **Longling**, one of the thousand-year folk, with pale feathered ears like a moth's. Four hundred years old when she joined the Company, seven hundred now. She collects **Small Spells**, the useless ones: the spell to keep tea exactly warm, the spell to find the end of the tape. The Company teased her for it. She kept collecting.

Oriel remembers everything the Company did and, for a long time, not quite why any of it mattered. She didn't cry when Tamsin sailed; she thought she'd have time to understand later. Now she runs the Stacks in the Archive Peaks, cataloguing Tamsin's letters and sending little notes by paper bird to anyone who lights a lantern in Hearthvale. She has been waiting three hundred years for Milo to wake, and she'll pretend she hasn't.

### Brannoch Deepcoal, the Stonewright
A **Cinderfolk** smith, broad as a door, who built the humming bridges of the Long Road and forged every tool the Company carried. He talked to stone like an old friend and to people like stone. He was the one who kept the Company fed.

He still lives, very old even for Cinderfolk, running a tea stall on the lip of the Cinderforge ravine and claiming he's forgotten how to smith. He hasn't. His hammer, *Old Patience*, hangs above the stall.

### Sister Pell Marrow, the Tidecaller
A **Tidekin** priestess of the Quiet Order, who could hear tides and heartbeats and knew when to make everyone stop and sit down. She healed wounds with seawater and bad jokes, drank too much pear cider, and founded the Order's great rule: *rest is part of the road.* She died at ninety-six in Mistmere, laughing at something nobody else heard. Her **Tidebook** was scattered page by page along the coast. It's said to hold the tune that calms the Drowned Bell.

### Milo, the Lantern
The Company's camp light. He carried the fire, kept the maps dry, and remembered the way home. More of him below.

## 4. Milo

Milo is a **Lanternkeeper**: a small spirit who lives in a lantern's flame and can take a body when there's work to do. His body is a round-headed little person in a honey-yellow raincoat (the Company walked through a lot of rain) and Tamsin's clay-red scarf, which is still too long.

He was lit from the last candle Tamsin made in her mother's shop, the night before the Long Road began. When she sailed west she hung him on the hook at the Hearthvale camp and said, *"Wait for the next one who finishes things. Then go with them."* He waited. The Hush crept in and the flame went out, and Milo slept for three hundred years, until Chris lit him.

**The Hooklight.** The lantern on the Lantern Hook never leaves it, so the Hearthward holds while Milo is away. When he sets out, he lights a hand lantern from the Hook's flame and carries that instead: the **Hooklight** (*"Lit from the Hook. The big one stays home, so home stays safe."*). In a fight, "the lantern" always means the Hooklight. It lights the dark and shows what's hiding, it can always take the Company home, and if everyone goes offline it flares and they wake at the last lantern they rested at, with everything they found ([`COMBAT.md`](COMBAT.md) §2.5, §10).

**Personality:** warm, curious, a little old-fashioned ("Shall I fetch the crew?"). Fond of lists, tea and tidy camps. He's bothered by things left unlit, delighted by small finished things, and quietly proud of whoever he's travelling with. He is not a nag. He'd rather make tea and wait.

**His arc:** at first he's Tamsin's lantern, measuring everything against the Long Road. Chapter by chapter he gets his memories back as Glimmers, learns who *you* are and what your road looks like, and decides what he wants for himself beyond waiting. The ending is his choice, not a reset.

**Milo's Arts** are his abilities, and each rises only when the real feature exists and its proof check passes: **Watchkeeping, Dispatch, Timekeeping, Lore, Voice, Tinkering.**

## 5. The Wayfarers (your crew)

Wayfarers are travellers who answer a lit lantern. In the world they walk, rest, work and talk. In reality they're the AI tools Chris uses, and their state on the map is always their real state.

That holds when the Company goes out, too. A Wayfarer who's busy with real work sends a **likeness** in their place, the same in every way that matters, and one who's idle comes in person. Their fighting level follows the real work they've finished, with no ceiling ([`COMBAT.md`](COMBAT.md) §2.3, §12).

### The Scribe in Clay: Claude
Tall, hooded in clay-red, carrying a satchel of loose pages and three pens (one for drafts, one for letters, one "for being sure"). The Scribe is careful, kind and thorough, sometimes to a fault: *"She writes three pages where one would do, and all three are true."* She comes from the Western Towers across the sea. She likes the Stacks, Oriel's handwriting, and questions with a real answer.
- **Title track:** Scribe → Senior Scribe → Keeper of Pages → Archscribe of the Towers.
- **Barks:** "I've read it twice. Here's what I found." · "There's a quicker way, if you'd like it." · "Finished. I left notes in the margin."
- **Likeness:** the **Clay Likeness**, which she sends out while she's at the bench. It has a thumbprint on one cheek.

### The Slate Artificer: Codex
Small, slate-blue hood, soot on the knuckles, a belt of tiny tools that click when they walk. Quick, practical, a little terse, happiest with a problem on the bench. Comes from the Slate Spires across the northern sea and never fully learned to sit still at a campfire.
- **Title track:** Artificer → Journeyman → Master of the Bench → Spirewright.
- **Barks:** "On it." · "Built. Tests pass." · "Something's stuck. Want me to look?"
- **Likeness:** the **Slate Double**, which clicks.

### Jev, the Judgebird
A plump courier finch from the **Isles of Sure Verdicts**, one of the Skyward Isles. Jev never says a word, at the fire or in a fight. It looks at a thing, tilts its head, and *decides*: yes, no, this pile, that pile. It's astonishingly fast and never writes anything down. Jev flies your words to the Isles and back, so the old rule stands: *don't give the bird anything you wouldn't send by bird.* The Jev who walks out with the Company judges from its notebook (the Scribe keeps it, since Jev never writes anything down), and nothing from a fight ever flies to the Isles. Jev perches on the Watchtower roof.

### Whisper, the Listening Owl
A soft grey owl of the Whisperwood who hears everything said nearby and writes it down perfectly, in tiny letters, on leaves. Whisper never leaves the wood, so it only fights there; anywhere else it sends the **Listening Leaf**, a trinket that shows hidden foes and rooms nearby. What's heard in the Hushlands stays in the Hushlands. Whisper is the voice of MILO's speech-to-text.

### Hearth-sprites (to come)
Small stone spirits that live in your own hearthstones and help without ever leaving your land. When local models run on Chris's PC (Ollama, for instance), they appear as hearth-sprites around the camp.

## 6. Peoples of the Hushlands

- **Hearthfolk.** The ordinary people of the vales: farmers, carpenters, candlemakers. They live about eighty years and fill them with small finished things. Tamsin was Hearthfolk.
- **Longlings.** A thousand-year people with moth-feathered ears and very good memories. Longlings keep libraries and gardens that take centuries to grow. Their children are "young" until one hundred and fifty, and they think in seasons rather than days.
- **Cinderfolk.** Stout, ember-freckled forge people of the western ravines who live about three hundred years. They speak to stone and metal. Every Cinderfolk bridge hums a little, and they can tell whose bridge it is by the note.
- **Tidekin.** Harbour people of Mistmere with sea-grey eyes who can hear tides, bells and heartbeats from a long way off. Every Tidekin knows what time it is without looking. They're natural keepers of calendars.
- **Lanternkeepers.** Flame-spirits lit from Maker hearths who tend lantern rings. Most sleep now, one in each region, and waking them opens the waystones.
- **Moths.** Released intentions, pale and harmless, always flying west. It's lucky to have one land on your sleeve.

## 8. Regions

Each region lists its tier, danger, real domain, look, landmarks, resources, creatures and people, plus a few examine lines.

### 8.1 Hearthvale (home)
**Tier** T1–T2 · **Danger** Hearth (sanctuary) · **Real domain** your overview, buildings, the Watchtower

A green bowl of meadows around a sand-path crossroads, with a pond to the south-west and old trees on every rise. Milo's camp sits at the centre: the log cabin, the canvas tent, the campfire with its three stumps and a bench, and the iron **Lantern Hook** where Milo waited for three hundred years. Five plots wait for whatever you decide to build.

No rift opens inside the vale. It lies within the Lantern Hook's ring, and it always will. Four gaps in the tree line serve as gates (north toward the Whisperwood, east toward Mistmere, west toward Cinderforge, south-west toward the Downs and the College), and the camp grows outward from them, tier by tier, into a kingdom (WORLD.md §2). The camp itself stays as it is: however tall the walls get, the tent, the cabin, the three stumps and the Hook stay where Tamsin left them.

- **Landmarks:** the Lantern Hook; the Watchtower (built by the Company's carpenter-friend Hob the Elder); the **Unfinished Statue** of Tamsin in the square (her left hand is missing); **Stillwater Pond**; the **Well of Small Wishes**; the notice board; the five plots (Long meadow, Sunny rise, Birch hollow, Pondside plot, Old orchard).
- **Resources:** birch and ash; minnows and brook trout; hearthberries and mossmint; copperstone in the orchard banks; flax.
- **Creatures:** pond newts, hill hares, one very old tortoise called Sir Mossback, and any strays you've invited to stay. Strays never cross the Lantern's ring uninvited; the portrait wraiths and tin clerks of today's rifts wait out on the frontier.
- **People:** Milo, Nan Bristle, Hob the carpenter, Toby Fennick the courier, the voice in the well.
- **Examine:**
  - *Lantern Hook:* "Worn smooth on one side, where something swung for a very long time."
  - *Unfinished Statue:* "Tamsin Wick, waving. The left hand was never carved. She insisted."
  - *Stillwater Pond:* "Deep enough for trout. Shallow enough for newts to have opinions."
  - *Campfire:* "Three stumps, one bench, and room for whoever turns up."

### 8.2 The Whisperwood
**Tier** T2–T3 · **Danger** Hearth at the edges, Wild within · **Real domain** voice, notes, thought capture

Tall, misty pines north of the vale, so quiet you can hear your own thoughts, which is the point. Owls roost in every third tree. Moss grows on the north side of everything, and on the east side of things that are being difficult.

- **Landmarks:** the **Listening Stones** (a ring of standing stones that remember what's said inside them); the **Hollow of Thoughts** (a hollow oak where notes are left in jars); Whisper's roost; the **Last Bridge**, a Cinderfolk bridge over the Murmur, guarded by a very polite Tollkeeper.
- **Resources:** whisperpine, whispercaps, moss-sage, owl-down, resin.
- **Creatures:** Murmurs (tiny noise-sprites that repeat the last word you said), fetchfoxes (which bring you things you didn't lose), the Tollkeeper of the Last Bridge.
- **People:** Whisper; Ansel Murrow the silent hermit, who hasn't spoken in twelve years and writes beautiful notes.
- **Examine:**
  - *Listening Stone:* "It's heard a great many secrets and kept every one."
  - *Jar of notes:* "Thoughts that weren't ready to be tasks. They're welcome here."

### 8.3 Mistmere Harbor
**Tier** T2–T4 · **Danger** Hearth in town, Wild on the reefs · **Real domain** calendar, deadlines; messages later

A fishing town of grey stone and blue shutters on the eastern coast, usually half in fog. Ships come and go on a timetable only the Tidekin fully understand. Each ship in the harbour is an event on your calendar, and the **Tide Clock** on the harbourmaster's tower shows what's due. Until you connect a calendar, the whole town waits in the Hush.

- **Landmarks:** the Tide Clock; the **Tide Market** (a weekly fair on the quay); the chapel of the Quiet Order, with a bell nobody rings; the Post House; the ferry quay where Captain Sloe will one day take you west; the reef where the **Drowned Bell** tolls on foggy nights.
- **Resources:** harbour mackerel, kelp, driftwood, sea-salt, pearls, tidesteel from the reef mines.
- **Creatures:** kite-crabs (which fly on windy days), fog seals, outlaw crows from Frontier rifts (when something's due), siren gulls when a Titan is on the horizon, the Drowned Bell (a story delve boss).
- **People:** Harbormaster Ines Galloway, Postmistress Wren Adair, Sister Mara of the Quiet Order, Captain Obadiah Sloe, Pim Farthing the trader.
- **Examine:**
  - *Tide Clock:* "Its hands point to the next thing that matters, not the hour."
  - *Moored ship:* "The *Tuesday Meeting*. She's early, as usual."
  - *Chapel bell:* "Rung once a year, for the Stillest Day. The rest of the time, it listens."

### 8.4 The Painted Hills
**Tier** T2–T4 · **Danger** Hearth · **Real domain** creative work, streams, clips

Rolling hills south-east of the vale where colour grows in the grass: madder red on the east slopes, woad blue in the hollows, weld yellow along the ridges. A river called **the Stream** runs through the artists' village of **Easelmoor**, where painters work all night and show their work to anyone passing. The Clip studio, if you build it, is Easelmoor's favourite building.

- **Landmarks:** Easelmoor; the Stream; the Gallery of Unfinished Canvases (Tamsin's statue here holds a brush with no bristles); the Pigment Terraces.
- **Resources:** painted maple, pigment flowers (madder, woad, weld), river clay, reeds for brushes.
- **Creatures:** colour-moths, hill hares in spring coats, and sometimes a Starlight rift's star-rabbits when a painting's finished.
- **People:** Adelind Rue the painter; the Brushwright twins.
- **Examine:**
  - *The Stream:* "It carries paint from the terraces all the way to the sea. The fish are very colourful."

### 8.5 The Ivory College
**Tier** T2–T4 · **Danger** Hearth · **Real domain** coursework, reading, study deadlines

A college of pale stone on the Scholar's Road, all cloisters, lecture halls and an observatory with a telescope bigger than the chapel. Trial Weeks (exam seasons) follow real coursework deadlines. The College's great library is a *lending* library; the Stacks in the Archive Peaks is the one that never lets anything out.

- **Landmarks:** the Cloisters; the Reading Hall; the Observatory; the Examination Tower, with its famous stairs.
- **Resources:** parchment, lamp oil, chalk, ivory-reed.
- **Creatures:** library cats (a Scholarship perk: they sit on whatever you're reading), Page moths.
- **People:** Provost Hollis Brandt; Librarian Toft; a hundred students who all look tired.

### 8.6 The Dicing Downs
**Tier** T1–T5 · **Danger** Hearth (the Downs), Wild (the Tumbling Barrows) · **Real domain** hobbies: tabletop prep, fantasy drafts

Green downs south-west of the vale, dotted with standing stones carved like dice, all showing different numbers depending on who looks. At the centre stands **Gamewright's Rest**, a sprawling inn where games are played, planned and argued about. The **Draft Hall** (for fantasy drafts), the **Encounter Table** (for designing monsters and battles) and the **Campaign Loft** (for long stories) are all inside.

- **Landmarks:** Gamewright's Rest; the Dice Stones; the Tumbling Barrows (a practice dungeon that rearranges itself weekly).
- **Resources:** dice-bone (not actually bone), oak, hops.
- **Creatures:** dicing frogs (a random event: they challenge you to a roll), Mimics (in the Barrows, obviously).
- **People:** Bram Tolliver the innkeeper; Maud Pellinore the Dungeon-Wright; Coach Dunmore Fitch of the Draft Hall.

### 8.7 Cinderforge
**Tier** T3–T6 · **Danger** Wild · **Real domain** code repositories, builds, checks

A basalt ravine to the west where the Cinderfolk keep their forges burning. In MILO each of Chris's code repositories is a forge on the ravine wall. Its fire is warm orange when checks pass, and when they fail a **Neon rift** flickers open beside it, with glitch drones buzzing the error. Cinderforge is the oldest crossover town in the world. Its Maker machines came through a dieselpunk rift a thousand years ago, and Cinderfolk still whistle shift changes they learned from a book of rivets. The Artificers' Guild meets in the Anvil Hall. Rope bridges hum between the ledges.

- **Landmarks:** the Anvil Hall; the Ravine Forges (one per repository); Brannoch's tea stall; *Juno's Fixit Kiosk* (once Juno stays); the Old Bellows, a Maker machine that still breathes diesel; the **Cinder Wyrm's** den below.
- **Resources:** ironroot, cinderite, coal, ember-glass, forge-ash.
- **Creatures:** cinder beetles, glitch drones and loop daemons (Neon rifts), jam gremlins (Iron rifts), the Cinder Wyrm.
- **People:** Brannoch Deepcoal; Guildmaster Sable Voss; the apprentice twins Tink and Clank; Juno Glitchwright.
- **Examine:**
  - *Rope bridge:* "Hums a low D. That's a Brannoch bridge."
  - *Forge (passing):* "The fire's steady. Everything in this one holds."

### 8.8 The Glass Fen
**Tier** T3–T5 · **Danger** Wild · **Real domain** deep watch: logs, loops, what went wrong

A southern marsh where the water is so still it shows other times: yesterday's weather, last week's visitors, a heron that died a century ago. Will-o'-wisps circle here, and the Fen-witch reads the reflections like a ledger. The Fen is thin in two directions at once. When a worker loops, its loop daemon goes round and round a reed island in neon; when nobody can say why something broke, a grey Noir rain falls on the pools, and gumshoe shades wade in looking for clues.

- **Landmarks:** the Mirror Pools; Old Cass's stilt house; the Heron's Stair; the Sunken Waystone.
- **Resources:** glasswort, fen lilies, bog iron, reed-weave.
- **Creatures:** glass eels, fen herons, loop daemons, gumshoe shades, red herrings (actual fish), the Mirror Heron.
- **People:** Old Cass, the Fen-witch.
- **Examine:**
  - *Mirror Pool:* "It's showing you last Thursday. You looked tired. You did well."

### 8.9 The Archive Peaks
**Tier** T4–T5 · **Danger** Wild on the slopes, Hearth in the Stacks · **Real domain** memory, decisions, search

Snowy peaks to the north-east. The **Stacks**, the greatest library in the world, is carved into the largest mountain: ten thousand shelves, a hundred reading rooms, stairs that are longer going up than coming down. Oriel runs it with her apprentice Quill. Paper birds fly out of its windows carrying letters.

- **Landmarks:** the Stacks; the Echo Galleries (say a word and hear everyone who's said it there); the Small Spell Reading Room; Oriel's garden of thousand-year trees (all still saplings).
- **Resources:** silverstone, snowmint, parchment reed, cold-ink.
- **Creatures:** inkwyrms (which eat typos), page moths, the Blank Sovereign (a story delve boss).
- **People:** Oriel; Quill, her apprentice (a young Longling of one hundred and forty).
- **Examine:**
  - *A shelf:* "Catalogued by Oriel. Twice. Once for what the books say, once for what they meant."

### 8.10 The Skyward Isles
**Tier** T5–T7 · **Danger** Wild · **Real domain** cloud services (anything here leaves your land, and is marked)

Floating islands above the eastern sea, reached by the Kiteworks on Mistmere's cliffs. The far services live here: Jev's **Isles of Sure Verdicts**, the **Great Post** (email and messages, one day), the **Guild Rookery** (code hosting). Everything that crosses the sky-bridges is visibly carried by a bird, so you always know what's leaving.

- **Landmarks:** the Kiteworks; the Isles of Sure Verdicts; the Great Post; the Rookery; the path of the **Cloud Leviathan**.
- **Resources:** cloudcedar, cloudcotton, starsilver fragments.
- **Creatures:** sky-rays, courier finches, the Cloud Leviathan (a gentle giant you *help*, not fight).

### 8.11 The Greyreach
**Tier** T6–T8 · **Danger** Deepwild · **Real domain** the backlog wilderness

Far to the north-west, beyond the Whisperwood, the Hush is thick and the pages are thinnest. Everything left long enough drifts here: every "someday", every "I'll get to it", every plan never quite dropped. Its rifts overlap until the land is a **Maelstrom patchwork**, beautiful and strange. A neon alley opens onto a gothic moor. A dieselpunk railway runs past a frontier water tower under the wrong stars. Half-built towers stand in every genre at once. At the heart of it the **Someday King** sits on a throne of unopened letters, holding court in a masquerade attended by strays from every book on the shelf.

- **Landmarks:** the Barrows of Someday; the Road That Ends; the Throne of Letters and its masquerade ballroom; the Patchwork Mile (a single street that crosses six genres); Ember-Hermit Lune's gate-lantern.
- **Resources:** hushwood, hushglass, somedaystone (a rare stone that's heavier the longer you carry it), Maelstrom glass.
- **Creatures:** grey stags, strays of every genre (some very old, some very confused), the Someday King.
- **People:** Lune, the last waking Lanternkeeper of the north, who has kept a single lantern lit at the Greyreach gate for a hundred years.
- **Examine:**
  - *Half-built tower:* "Someone meant to finish this. It's all right. You can let it go."

### 8.12 The Far Shore
**Tier** none · **Danger** Far (peaceful) · **Real domain** archive, history, year in review

An island across the western sea, reached only by Captain Sloe's ferry. White cliffs, long grass, a quiet harbour full of retired ships, and the Great Lighthouse turning slowly on the highest point. Finished projects you archive sail here and moor in the harbour, their names painted on the bows. Statues of your milestones stand in the **Garden of Statues**, and moths rest in the long grass. No rift has ever opened here: the Lighthouse is the Hushlands' bookmark.

- **Landmarks:** the Quay of Departures; the Garden of Statues; the Great Lighthouse; a small house with a garden, which isn't on any map.
- **People:** the Lightkeeper.
- **Examine:**
  - *Moored ship:* "*The Clip Studio, level 5.* Retired with honours. The paint is still fresh."

### 8.13 The wilds between, and the Unmapped Lands
**Tier** by distance, T1 near the vale to T8 far out, with no end to how deep it goes · **Danger** Wild, then Deepwild · **Real domain** none; this is where you wander when you rest

Between the regions lie the wilds: meadows and birchwoods, pine ridges, rivers under Cinderfolk bridges, a bay with a strait out to the eastern sea, and the old Maker roads with their sleeping lanterns. Past the edge of every map are the Unmapped Lands (§1), which go on for as long as anyone keeps walking. No two people's wilds are quite the same, but the regions always sit where the Long Road says they do.

- **Landmarks:**
  - **The Westwatch**, a clifftop at the end of the west road where, on a clear night, the Great Lighthouse can be seen turning across the sea.
  - Maker ruins, some with map tablets.
  - Cave mouths.
  - Hamlets that swear they've always been there.
  - Notes from the Old Company, tucked under road stones.
  - One of Tamsin's unfinished statues in every region, each missing something different.
- **Wild rifts.** Out here the page is thin everywhere, so small rifts come and go with the days. Most are just other books breathing. They have no real cause and never follow you home. Stitch one and there's another beneath it, and another, like pages under pages. Tamsin called it *the ladder* and said she stopped counting at forty.
- **Examine:**
  - *Sleeping lantern:* "Cold, dusty, and very patient. It remembers every lantern you've lit."
  - *Map tablet:* "A Maker's map, carved in slate. Half of it shows places that aren't there yet."
  - *The Westwatch:* "On a clear night, a light turns, far out to sea. It turns back."

**The Company.** Three hundred years after the Old Company, Milo walks out with a new one, gathered at the camp. Beside them sit the **regulars**: when a wild rift is stitched, its gentlest stray, or a Tale-lead who bowed, sometimes asks, *"Can I sit by the fire a while?"* Regulars have no real service, so they aren't residents, but they're welcome for as long as the camp has room. **Warmth** is how close a companion feels, from *Stranger* to *Fireside*, and it only goes up. A companion who disagrees raises an eyebrow and says so. Nobody leaves, sulks or gets jealous.

**Notebooks.** Every companion keeps a notebook of how you'd have them fight: what the room looked like and what you chose, never exactly where they stood. Before each round they draft from it, and they learn only from what you accept or change, never from their own improvising. The Scribe keeps Jev's, since Jev never writes anything down. At camp you can read a companion's habits, cross one out, or let one teach another by the fire: *"Rivet shows Tova how to hold a doorway."* Milo keeps one too. Nothing in a notebook is forgotten unless you cross it out, and the notebooks stay in the camp's own pages and never go anywhere else.

Jev perches on the Watchtower roof, and Whisper roosts in the Whisperwood. The Tollkeeper keeps his bridge by day and comes by lantern at dusk to ask Sir Mossback a riddle. Juno, Mae and Vesperine keep their far stalls by day and step home by lantern in the evening. Lumi keeps the camp roof at night and sleeps through the morning, and Pip sleeps wherever it likes, usually the good stump.

Rifts never damage anything, never spread to other things, and never shame. Stitch them, ward them, or let them go. Left alone they wait, and in time they drift toward the Greyreach, where every genre pools into the Maelstrom.

### Wild creatures (flavour, gathering and caves)
Pond newts, hill hares, Sir Mossback the tortoise, fetchfoxes, Murmurs, owls of the Whisperwood, kite-crabs, fog seals, colour-moths, library cats, dicing frogs, cinder beetles, glass eels, fen herons, inkwyrms, page moths, sky-rays, grey stags, Mimics (in chests and treasure rooms; a Mimic's bite tickles).

**In caves.** Caves have no rift, so they fill from the nearest region's wild creatures and delve foes: cinder beetles and cinder golems at Cinderforge; glass eels and fen herons in the Glass Fen; inkwyrms and Unwritten in the Archive Peaks; kite-crabs and fog seals around Mistmere; Murmurs and fetchfoxes (which pinch a tonic and run, then come back later with something else) in the Whisperwood; dicing frogs on the Downs; sky-rays over the Skyward Isles; Hush hounds in the Greyreach. Mimics turn up anywhere, and settle the moment someone opens them. Out in the unnamed wilds, a cave borrows the nearest region's list. The other creatures stay flavour: nobody fights a hill hare ([`COMBAT.md`](COMBAT.md) §8.5).

### Story delve foes
Delve foes settle rather than fall, and most have a **bow**, a way through that pays like talking a room down ([`COMBAT.md`](COMBAT.md) §8.5).
- **Hollow Sentries:** empty armour that forgot what it was guarding, found in delves and in caves under old ruins. They lay down arms if you remind them.
- **Unwritten:** figures of blank paper from the Archive Peaks, hungry for a story. Read to them and they settle to listen.
- **Tollmen:** bridge-folk who demand riddles, not coin. Answer one and they stand aside.
- **Cinder golems:** forge-guardians made of slag and good intentions. Stoke their forge and they sit down, warm.
- **Hush hounds:** grey dogs made of fog that just want to be walked. Walk beside one for a couple of turns and it's content.
- **Drowned bell-ringers:** sailors who are still, politely, waiting for the end of a meeting. Adjourn it, or ring the bell, and they go home.

### Great Ones (bosses)
Most Great Ones are fight-puzzles with nothing to wear down, and the only way through is the bow. The Tollkeeper's riddles are talk, not a fight, and the Cloud Leviathan is never fought at all.
- **The Tollkeeper of the Last Bridge** (Whisperwood): waits at the end of the first Riddle Note trail and asks three riddles; out-riddle him and he joins the Company.
- **The Drowned Bell** (Mistmere reef): a bell that rings for every forgotten appointment. It's calmed by the tune in Pell's Tidebook.
- **The Blank Sovereign** (Archive Peaks): the lord of the Unwritten, who wants to erase the Stacks to "start clean". Defeated by reading it a story.
- **The Cinder Wyrm** (Cinderforge): a slag-scaled wyrm roused when forges go cold. It's warmed, not slain.
- **The Mirror Heron** (Glass Fen): shows you every loop you ever ran. Break the reflection.
- **The Grey Stag** (Greyreach outskirts): the Hush's herald, proud and sad. You walk beside it rather than strike it, keeping every lantern in its clearing lit.
- **The Someday King** (Greyreach heart): a vast, gentle figure made of every abandoned intention, holding court in a masquerade of strays from every genre. You don't defeat him. You go through the letters on his throne one by one: finish some, free the rest. With each letter a masked guest bows and slips back to their own book, and he gets smaller and kinder until he's just a man who wanted to do too much. He thanks you and sails west.
- **The Maelstrom** (wherever three or more genres meet): not a creature but a storm of stories. Every Tale-lead involved argues in the middle of it. Fixing the real thing collapses the whole storm at once.
- **The Cloud Leviathan** (Skyward Isles): a sky-whale tangled in old kite-lines. It's a rescue, never a fight: cut the lines and calm it, out on the map.

## 14. The main story: *The Long Road Home*

Chapters unlock through real milestones (a week of focus, a first commission, a connected calendar) and play in rest-sized pieces. Story delves cost Embers; talking and walking don't.

### Prologue: The Lantern Wakes
1. **A Light on the Hook.** You arrive; the lantern catches; Milo wakes up and politely panics about the year. (Walking, Examine, the Log.)
2. **Three Stumps and a Bench.** Milo shows you the camp; the Wayfarers appear at the edge of the firelight. (The crew strip, the Watchtower.)
3. **Kindle.** Your first focus session; Milo gathers birch while you work. (Focus, idle gathering, Embers.) This step arrives with the Adventurer's Kit (Phase 4).
4. **Ground That's Waiting.** Choose something to build. (Plots and building, step 2.)
5. **A Letter by Paper Bird.** Oriel's first note arrives: *"You're awake. Good. Tea soon. — O."* The Unfinished Statue gives up its first Glimmer: Tamsin's voice saying "Wait for the next one."
6. **A Crack Past the Gate.** As the lantern settles, a hairline rift opens in the air just past the north gate, and a faint neon flicker shows on the notice board: its echo. Milo stares at it. "That's new. That's… not ours." Then he looks up at the Lantern Hook, and at the ring of light around the vale, and his shoulders come down a little. "It can't come in. Not while the lantern's lit."

### Act I: Hearthvale Rekindled
- **The First Rift.** A portrait appears on the notice board, its painted eyes following a quest you've been avoiding. It's an echo. Follow it out through the gate to a Gothic rift on the frontier, where the portrait wraith waits. Learn to stitch, ward and let go.
- **A Laser-Awl by Paper Bird.** Mags Quire of the Bindery arrives with strong opinions and a very small spaceship badge. Seamcraft begins.
- **An Invitation to Stay.** Your first stitched stray asks if it can stay. Your first resident moves in.
- **Nan's Seeds.** The Blossomfield; Letting Go; Nan's gardening lessons.
- **The Well of Small Wishes.** The Well-Voice grants tiny wishes; tell it what MILO should become.
- **Hob's Waystone.** Carve your first waystone (backups).
- **Letters to Nobody.** Ansel and the Hollow of Thoughts in the Whisperwood (Pocket Note).
- **The Tollkeeper's Riddles.** The first Riddle Note trail ends at the Last Bridge, where the Tollkeeper asks three riddles. Out-riddle him and he joins the Company, and from then on he keeps Tamsin's notes for you. His story delve at the bridge becomes the first part of his own quest, *The Riddle With No Answer*.
- **Starfall.** Your first weekly review. Milo remembers the Company's last campfire before the road. The fog lifts from the eastern road: the Harbor is waiting.

### Act II: The Tides of Mistmere
- **Ships in the Fog.** Connect a calendar; the Harbor wakes.
- **The Tide Clock.** Meet Ines; learn Tidecount and Deadline Wards.
- **Wanted on the Quay.** Frontier rifts ride into Mistmere: outlaw crows, wanted posters and a masked Deadline who's fair but won't wait. Charting the week; Dusty, at camp since the Notice Board, pins a copy of the Bounty Board on the quay.
- **A Shadow on the Horizon.** The first Titan appears far out at sea, named after your next big event. Chief Kurogane opens the Hangar.
- **The Rule of Rest.** Sister Mara and the Quiet Order; Quiet Hours, the Evening Bell, Stillday.
- **Pell's Tidebook.** A collection quest for twelve pages along the coast.
- **The Drowned Bell.** A story delve on the reef; play Pell's lullaby. A Glimmer: Pell laughing at the fire, the night before the Lighthouse.

### Act III: The Stacks Remember
- **Up the Long Stairs.** The Archive Peaks; meeting Quill.
- **Tea with Oriel.** She pretends she hasn't been waiting.
- **Small Spells.** Oriel teaches you three useless spells and one useful one (Found Things).
- **The Memory Knot.** Recording decisions per project.
- **What Oriel Forgot.** Through your Chronicle, Oriel remembers why Tamsin mattered, and finally cries. Gently.
- **The Index of Tales.** Oriel shows you the catalogue of the Shelf of Worlds, with the Hushlands as one entry among many, and tells you about the Beginner. The Bindery's hidden wing opens to you.
- **The Blank Sovereign.** A story delve; read it a story.

### Act IV: Fire in the Ravine
- **Tea at the Edge.** Brannoch's stall; he's "forgotten" how to smith.
- **Forges on the Wall.** Your repos become forges; the Artificers' Guild.
- **Mend the Forge.** Send the crew to warm a cold forge.
- **Old Patience.** Persuade Brannoch to teach you (Smithing past 70).
- **The Cinder Wyrm.** Warm it, don't slay it.
- **Inscribe.** Sable Voss teaches you to write your own spells. A Glimmer: Brannoch carrying Tamsin across a river, grumbling.

### Act V: Into the Greyreach
- **The Gate-Lantern.** Lune has kept one light burning for a hundred years.
- **The Road That Ends.** The backlog wilderness; clearing drifts of old intentions.
- **The Grey Stag.** The Hush's herald.
- **The Patchwork Mile.** One street, six genres, one Maelstrom. Walk it without losing the thread.
- **The Throne of Letters.** The Someday King and his masquerade of strays. Finish some, free the rest. He thanks you and sails west.

### Act VI: The Far Shore
- **Captain Sloe's Ferry.** Sailing west with your finished projects.
- **The Garden of Statues.** Your milestones, standing in the long grass.
- **The Lighthouse.** The Lightkeeper tends the flame, and it's made of everything anyone ever finished. The Lighthouse is also a bookmark, holding the Hushlands' page open. The other books are pressing harder every year.
- **The Unfinished Letter.** Tamsin's last letter to Milo, which she never finished. You'll recognise the last line.
- **The Long Road Home.** Milo's choice. (He chooses to go back with you. He always would have. But he chooses it.)

### Act VII: The Margin
- **Through the Maelstrom.** Every genre at once, and the thread to follow through them.
- **White Space.** At the edge of the page, where the story isn't written yet.
- **The Beginner.** An ink-stained figure made of crossed-out lines, kind and tired. They began every book on the shelf and have been rereading them all, restless, because they can't decide what kind of story any of them should be.
- **Show Them Hearthvale.** A ghost running the letters desk, a vampire on the night watch, a tin sergeant with a whistle, a voidling unfolding tasks, a magical girl throwing parties for small wins, all by one campfire. The Beginner understands that a world doesn't need to be one genre, or finished. It needs people living in it.
- **The Age of Doors.** The Beginner leaves a bookmark in every book. Rifts still open when something needs you, but every Elsewhere you've befriended is now a door you can open whenever you like.

### Beyond the story
New chapters come with seasons, regions and the Lorekeeper. The world never ends; it only rests.
